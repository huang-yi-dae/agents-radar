## 3. Trend Signal Analysis

The clearest signal today is the commoditization of agent memory and skills. The top movers are not another chat UI; they are "skill routers" like [reverse-skill](https://github.com/zhaoxuya520/reverse-skill), "memory hubs" like [TencentDB-Agent-Memory](https://github.com/TencentCloud/TencentDB-Agent-Memory), and "context persistence" layers like [claude-mem](https://github.com/thedotmack/claude-mem) and [mem0](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0). The community is moving from "can we build an agent?" to "how do we make agents retain what they learn and behave like domain experts?"

A second wave centers on locally runnable frontier-class inference. [AirLLM](https://github.com/lyogavin/airllm)'s 70B-on-4GB-GPU approach and [antirez/ds4](https://github.com/antirez/ds4)'s DeepSeek 4 inference engine both respond to GPU cost and memory constraints. The appearance of DeepSeek-native tools like [DeepSeek-Reasonix](https://github.com/esengine/DeepSeek-Reasonix) also follows a familiar industry pattern: a major open-weights release (DeepSeek 4) triggers an immediate wave of local inference engines, coding agents, and evaluation/fine-tuning companions.

A third direction is the "skill package" distribution format. Instead of standalone apps, developers now ship domain expertise as installable modules for existing AI coding clients—security skills, last-30-days research skills, and Korean-language agent skills all follow this pattern. Finally, RAG is moving beyond vector-only retrieval: [Graphify](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify) and similar projects push deterministic AST/reasoning-based approaches, while on-device RAG projects like LEANN emphasize storage compression and privacy. The center of gravity is shifting from generic LLM wrappers to specialized, persistent, context-aware agent infrastructure.
